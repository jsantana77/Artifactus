# DM Table

Battle map, fog of war, initiative, dice and drag-and-drop inventory for running a tabletop game.
Players follow along on their own phones; everything syncs through one Netlify Function + Netlify Blobs.

## Deploy
1. Put these files in a GitHub repo (keep the folder layout). To update an existing deploy, replace the files and commit.
2. In Netlify: Add new site, Import from Git, pick the repo. Build settings are read from `netlify.toml`.
3. Open the site, choose "New table", set a DM PIN. Share the table code or the "Copy player link" button.

Existing tables keep working after an update.

## Layout
- `public/` the web app (index, dm, play pages; common.js has the map view; dice.js the roller)
- `netlify/functions/api.mjs` the API (state, map, fog mask, token images, initiative, DM PIN check)

## Notes
- Fog of war is drawn in the browser. The full map image is still sent to player devices, so a determined player could find it.
- Every player roll is sent to the DM's roll feed. The DM chooses whether players can see the feed, and whether the DM's own rolls are shared.
- Map sharpness: uploaded map pictures keep up to 4096 pixels on the long side (and no more than about 14 million pixels in total, which is what phones can draw), saved as WebP where the browser can do that, otherwise JPEG, and always within the ~4 MB upload limit. The picture is never enlarged, so a map can only be as sharp as the file it came from. To swap in a sharper copy of a map that's already set up, use Replace on its location: if it's the same picture at a different size, the grid, outlines, fog and token positions stay lined up (the grid is scaled to the new pixel size); a different picture resets them.
- Tap-to-roll on NPC and enemy stat cards, the same way a player's sheet works. Every stat card (in the NPCs & Enemies tab, the combat window, and a token's Stats button) has a **roll bar** at the top (it stays in view as you scroll) with the last result, a Disadv. / Normal / Adv. switch (it applies to the next d20 and then goes back to Normal, like on a player sheet), and a **Players see: yes/no** switch that is the same setting as the Dice panel's "show my rolls to players" box, so a roll shows in the roll feed under the creature's own name (the token's name, like Goblin 2) only when you share. What you can tap: each ability score for a check, and under it that ability's saving throw (using the bonus the stat block lists, such as Dex +4, or just the modifier when none is listed); Initiative; each skill in the Skills line; every attack's to-hit and its damage (all of an attack's damage parts roll together, listed by type, and a small crit button rolls it as a critical hit; a natural 20 on the to-hit makes that attack's next damage roll a critical hit automatically, the dice doubled and not the bonus); saving-throw effects like a breath weapon (damage); and spells (a spell attack at the creature's spell attack bonus, plus damage or healing dice, worked out from the spell's description in the spell library, shown beside the spell's name, and the spell still opens as before; damage at higher spell levels isn't worked out). Stat blocks written the old way, as free text, roll too: "+4 to hit" and "5 (1d6+2) slashing damage" in the text become buttons, and so does dice written in a special ability's description. In the combat window each creature keeps its own roll bar, and it comes back after the card is redrawn (an HP change, say).
- Attacks, abilities and spells on NPC and enemy stat blocks: besides the free-text boxes, each library entry now has structured parts that read like a real stat block. **Attacks** (name, kind: melee, ranged, melee or ranged, spell attack, saving-throw effect or other; to-hit bonus; reach or range; up to four damage parts, each with dice and a type, and the average worked out for you as you type, so 1d6+2 shows as 5 (1d6+2); an optional saving throw with its ability, DC and what happens on a success; extra effects). **Special abilities and actions** (traits, Multiattack and other actions, bonus actions, reactions and legendary actions, each with how often it works and, for legendary actions, the action cost; they appear under the right heading on the card). **Spellcasting** (ability, spell save DC, spell attack bonus, caster level, slots per level, or innate spellcasting with uses per day; a Fill button works the DC and attack bonus out from the ability and the challenge rating; spells are added one by one or searched in the spell library, and each spell on the card opens to show what it does, grouped by level with slots, or by how often for innate casters). The old text boxes are still there, under **More text**, and still show on the card, so existing stat blocks look exactly as before. The structured parts show on the stat card, in the combat window, and from the token's Stats button, and the list in the library shows a summary (1 attack · 3 spells). Session plans remember which library entry a stat block came from, so putting one on the map from the plan links the token to its full stat block, and saving a plan stat block to the library links it too.
- Player sheet on a second screen: on a wide screen, the player page has a **Sheet on 2nd screen** button (next to Map on 2nd screen). It opens the whole panel area in its own window: Initiative, Goals, Campaign, Items, Sheet, Spells, Notes and Dice, with their buttons along the bottom, so everything (typing into the sheet, tap-to-roll, spell slots, notes, dice) works exactly as before while the map gets the whole main window. A small note under the map offers Show it, Bring it back here and Fit map. A panel is always showing over there (its button doesn't close it). The map window and the sheet window can be open at the same time. Closing the window, or Bring back, returns everything to the main window. Because the sheet can now live in another window, the "don't redraw while the player is typing" checks look at the window the sheet is in, and the page keeps polling while any of its windows is visible. The button is hidden on phone-size screens.
- Players' stats, abilities and spells for the DM: every player has a read-only overview card made from their character sheet: armor class, speed, initiative, proficiency, passive Perception, hit points on the token, ability scores and modifiers, saves, skills, attacks, spellcasting ability with save DC and attack bonus, spell slots left, their whole spell list grouped by level (with how many slots are left at each level, and a tap on any spell shows what it does from the spell library, plus the player's own note on it), features and traits, proficiencies and languages, and personality and backstory. It appears in four places: at the top of the Party tab there is a **party at a glance** table (one row per player: class, HP, AC, speed, initiative, passive Perception, spell DC and attack bonus, slots left; tap a name for the full card); each player's card on the Party tab starts with their Overview (above the editable spell book and character sheet); every player token in the token list has a **Sheet** button (and the token's Edit dialog has a matching button) that opens the overview from any tab; and the combat window's player cards now include the spell list, features and proficiencies (the spell list is open by default when it's their turn). Which sections you open or close is remembered while the cards are redrawn. The editable character sheet on each Party card has the same hit points box a player sees (current and maximum, Damage and Heal with temp HP absorbing first, and Set HP directly); it reads and writes the character's token, so it always matches the initiative list and the combat window, and the overview shows a Hit Points line.
- Combat window: the Initiative card has a **2nd screen** button that opens the initiative tracker in its own window (like the map), with everyone's stat blocks beside it. Everything on the tracker works over there (Start combat, Back, Next turn, Sort, Roll NPCs & enemies, Add everyone, typing initiative, removing someone). The stat panel has two modes: **Follow the turn** shows the full stat block of whoever's turn it is, and **Everyone** shows a card for every combatant in initiative order, with the current turn outlined and scrolled into view. Enemies and NPCs show their stat block from the NPCs & Enemies library (a token remembers which entry it came from); players show a card made from their character sheet (armor class, speed, initiative, proficiency, passive Perception, abilities, saves, skills, spell save DC and attack bonus, spell slots left, attacks); a token with no stat block gets a small card with a picker to link one. Every card has the creature's live HP with a bar and quick -5, -1, +1, +5 buttons (heals stop at max, damage stops at 0) and an HP... button for the full dialog. Click a name in the initiative list to pin that card (click again, or press Next turn, to follow the turn again). The map window and the combat window can be open at the same time. Close the window, or press Bring back, and the tracker returns to the sidebar.
- Resizing tokens and the grid: select a token on the map (tap it, or its name in the token list) and drag the gold handles. The corner handle scales the whole token and keeps its shape; on objects, the right and bottom handles stretch just the width or the height, so a shelf can be made exactly as long as the one drawn on the map. The opposite side stays where it is and a readout shows the size as you drag. Sizes follow the grid: they land on quarter squares and are pulled toward whole squares, so "exactly one square" is easy to hit; hold Alt (Option) on a computer to size freely, and Shift on the corner to stretch freely. Objects can be 0.1 to 40 squares across and people 0.25 to 8. The Edit dialog takes any size too (such as 0.35), with **One square**, Half, Double and Keep proportions buttons. Items start as one square (Potion, Scroll, Key, Treasure). With **Snap tokens** on, tokens land by their EDGES, not just their middles: a 1x1 item fills one square, a 2-wide shelf sits between grid lines, a size-2 creature sits on a grid corner, a half-square item fits a half-square slot, and a token turned at an angle uses the middle of a square. This works on flat and perspective grids. Players don't see the handles.
- Developer note: object tokens use the CSS class `otok`. The class `obj` belongs to the Objectives cards (background, padding, a gold left border), and an earlier version put `obj` on object tokens too, which gave them a minimum size and a gold crescent. Keep the two separate.
- NPCs & Enemies: a tab (between Locations and Campaign) holding a library of stat blocks for the whole campaign, for the DM's eyes only. Each entry is an NPC or an enemy and has a full stat block (armor class, hit points and dice, speed, challenge, the six abilities with their modifiers worked out, saves, skills, resistances, senses, languages, traits, actions, and bonus actions, reactions and legendary actions), tags, and notes. NPCs also have a role, what they want, a secret, and their voice. The list can be searched (name, tags, role), filtered to NPCs or enemies, and sorted A to Z, by challenge rating, or by when it was last edited; entries can be duplicated and deleted. **View card** shows a clean stat block for reading at the table. **Add to map** puts any number on the map as tokens with their hit points and Dexterity initiative bonus, optionally into initiative and optionally hidden, and each token remembers its entry: a **Stats** button on its row in the token list (and in its edit dialog) opens the card from any tab. In a session plan, NPCs and stat blocks have **Save to library**, and the NPC and enemy sections have **Add from library**. Players never see any of it.
- Fine grids: the square size can go down to 3 pixels, so a map with many small squares works. Next to the slider there's a number box (steps of 0.05) and an **Across** box: type how many squares fit across the map (map makers usually say, for example, "45 x 45") and the size is worked out for you. Grid lines are thin on small squares and the usual 2 pixels on big ones; **Line width** overrides that (and Auto puts it back), and the Move grid nudge step shrinks with the squares. These settings are saved with each location and scale if you replace a map with a sharper copy.
- Moving along the grid: when the map has a grid showing, a player taps **Move**, presses their own token and drags along the squares to trace the route they want (or taps squares to add to it; dragging back over the route shortens it, and Undo and Clear are there too). Each square is 5 feet, diagonals included. In combat the route can't be longer than their speed minus what they've already moved this turn, and **Dash** doubles the speed for the turn; out of combat (Free explore) there's no limit. Speed comes from the number the DM puts on the token (Edit token > Speed), otherwise from the character sheet's Speed, otherwise 30 feet. Routes can't enter fog, areas outside the outlined grid, the edge of the map, or a square with a visible enemy, and can't end on another character. The DM sees each route drawn on the map with its length and warnings (fog, a skipped square, the token having moved, ending on someone, or going through a hidden token, an enemy or an object), and approves or denies; Auto-approve only takes routes with no warnings. Approving moves the token to the end of the route and counts the feet against that turn. A map with no grid still uses tap-a-spot.
- Session plans: in Campaign > Sessions, every session has a **Plan** side (for prep, before the game) and a **Log** side (for what happened). The plan is for the DM only and is never shown to players or included in a player recap. It has sections for the goal and strong start, beats and scenes (tick them off as you play them), secrets and clues, locations (link a place to a saved map and jump to it), NPCs (wants, secret, voice; add to map), dialogue and read-aloud text, enemies with full stat blocks (ability modifiers work themselves out; **Add to map** puts any number of them on the map with their hit points, an initiative bonus and an option to hide them), rewards, and contingency notes. The full DM report in Word can include each session's plan, so it can be printed as a prep sheet.
- Second screen: the DM page and the player page each have a **Map on 2nd screen** button that opens the map in its own window. Drag that window to another monitor (or tap Full screen). The map stays live and fully usable there. For the DM the whole map toolbar goes with it (upload, grid, fog, perspective, shapes), and tokens can be dragged on that screen, while the main window keeps all the other tabs and panels. For players the main window gives its whole area to the panel they pick (sheet, spells, notes, initiative...) and the map window shows whose turn it is. Closing the window, or the Bring back button, returns the map. It works on one computer with two screens; the window is opened by the page, so pop-ups must be allowed.
- Animated maps: a location's map can be a short looping video (MP4 or WebM) instead of a picture. It plays silently and repeats on the DM's screen and on every player's phone, with the grid, fog, tokens, pings and everything else drawn over it. Upload it as the location's map. Videos must be under about 4 MB (the hosting platform limits an upload to about 6 MB once encoded), so keep loops short and export them at a modest size, for example 1280 pixels wide at a low bitrate. A phone in low-power mode may need one tap on the map to start the video.
- Object tokens: besides Player, NPC and Enemy, the DM can add Objects: furniture (tables, benches, beds), boats (canoe to ship), buildings (house, cabin, shop, walls), tents and camp, dungeon props (doors, chests, traps, pits), nature and treasure. Pick from ready-made presets with sensible sizes. Boats, houses and tents are drawn as top-down footprints (hull, roof, tent) you can see the map through; other objects are solid pieces with an optional picture. Each can be resized in grid squares, turned, recoloured, hidden from players, given HP (destructible), and have its name shown inside, below, or hidden. Objects stay out of initiative unless added by hand, and can be brought along or left behind when switching locations.
- Tap to roll: on the character sheet, tap any bonus to roll a d20 with that modifier: skills, saving throws, ability checks, initiative, spell attack, attacks and their damage (a natural 20 doubles the damage dice), and death saves. Advantage and disadvantage are one tap. The result shows in a bar that stays at the top, goes to the roll feed for the DM, and an initiative roll also goes into the DM's initiative list.
- Player characters: anyone with an account has a character library (`home.html`). A character is created and edited on its own page (`character.html`): the full sheet, spells and notes, with no campaign needed. Joining a campaign is a separate step: from the home page, enter the campaign code and pick a character. The DM sees a Join request (Accept, Decline, or Auto-accept), the token appears on the map, and the player is logged in as that character automatically, with no PIN. The character's sheet stays in the player's library, so it follows them between sessions. If a player leaves or the DM removes the token, only the DM can control that token afterwards.
- DM accounts: DMs create an account (name and password) on the front page, then see their own list of campaigns (`home.html`). Each campaign is a separate table with its own code, players, maps and notes, and DMs can't open each other's. Passwords are stored as salted hashes. Older tables with a code and PIN still work, and can be added to an account from the campaigns page.
- Player logins: on the Party tab, "Player login" gives a character a PIN (stored only as a hash). Players pick their character and enter the PIN; the sheet, notes, spells, initiative and roll names then belong to that login. Characters with no PIN stay open to anyone with the table code. Setting a new PIN signs out old devices.
- Character sheet: players fill in identity, abilities, saves, skills, combat numbers, attacks, features, backstory and coins. Modifiers, proficiency, saves, skills, initiative, passive Perception and spell DC are worked out automatically. Players send HP changes to the DM, whose screen applies them.
- Player sheets (spell slots, known spells, notes) are saved on the server per character. The DM can edit slots and spells and read the notes.
- Spell library: the DM imports a CSV, TSV or JSON list (Party tab, "Spell library"). Players search it and add spells to their sheets. To ship a default list with the app, add `public/spells.json` (an array of objects with name, level, school, time, range, comp, dur, classes, desc).
- Item lists and HP for player characters are still visible to every player at the table.
- Perspective grid: for maps seen at an angle or fading into a horizon. Choose Grid > Perspective on the Map tab, drag the four blue corners onto the floor of the map, and set how many squares fit across and deep. Squares shrink toward the far side, tokens and pings shrink with them, and snapping follows the grid. It is saved per location.
- Pings and movement: players can ping a spot on the map (everyone sees it for a few seconds). They can also ask to move their character: on their turn in combat, or any time when the DM turns on Free explore. Spots must be revealed (not under fog) and inside any grid shapes. Requests go to the DM's Move requests list and only happen when approved (or when Auto-approve is on).
- Locations tab (DM): save several maps as locations. Each remembers its map image, grid (size, position, dimming, shape), fog and where every token stood. Switch from the Location dropdown on the Map tab or the Locations tab; choose which tokens come along. An older single map can be saved as a location with one click.
- Campaign tab (DM): game details (overview, premise, themes, setting, factions, table rules, secrets) and a session log (recap, accomplished, learned, earned, people and places, open threads, private notes). Each section can be shared with players, who see it under Campaign. Export a Word (.docx) report: a full DM version or a player recap of shared items only. Secrets and DM notes can never be shared.
- Objectives are DM-managed; each one is hidden or shown to players.
- Grid tools: dim the grid, slide it under the map, and outline shapes so the grid stops at the edges of irregular maps.
- If a page can't start (usually a file missing or out of date after an update), a red banner at the top says so and names the problem. Fix it by uploading the complete project files and refreshing with Ctrl+Shift+R.
